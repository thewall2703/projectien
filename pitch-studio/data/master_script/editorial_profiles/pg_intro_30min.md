# Editorial Profile: PG Introduction, Working Graduates, 30-Minute Zoom

This profile contains presentation-specific decisions. Apply it together with the
durable editorial context. It overrides general preferences where it is more specific.

## Delivery brief

- Employee presenter.
- Audience: working graduates aged 24–30 who do not yet know Masters' Union.
- Channel: Zoom with the deck.
- Total slot: 30 minutes, including pauses and at least 5 minutes for questions.
- Spoken target: approximately 25 minutes; the word budget is a ceiling and pacing guide.
- Primary programme: PGP in Technology & Business Management.
- Active content authority: `Brand Deck Master Script v2.pdf`, represented by
  `master_script.v2.json`.

## Required route and coverage

Use the v2 postgraduate route:

1. founding argument;
2. purpose, university status and governance;
3. founder proof;
4. postgraduate outcomes;
5. learning model and its components;
6. relevant postgraduate programme and one admissions action.

Required content:

- PlaySuper founders joined the postgraduate programme at age twenty-seven, with the
  venture outcome labelled accurately;
- at least two source-backed career transitions by function;
- the Cohort of 2025's reported 3.03x average increase over pre-programme salaries,
  framed as historical rather than promised;
- the university-status bill claim, without adding enactment, degree rights or other
  legal conclusions;
- concrete Board of Governors credentials, with each credential earning its space;
- exact faculty composition: 40% practitioners, 30% full-time PhD faculty and 30%
  visiting international faculty;
- 16-month TBM for experienced professionals versus the 24-month Young Leaders Cohort
  for fresh graduates;
- one next action: talk to Admissions about the next TBM cohort.

Do not invent an application date, URL, response time, fee, eligibility rule or claim
about compatibility with employment. Keep missing application details in a nonspoken
presenter note.

## Approved editorial decisions

### Opening and founding section

- Start directly with the doctor and hospital analogy from the source.
- Do not open with “We don't prepare students for the world. We let them build it.”
- Do not replace the founding decision with dates, an agenda or audience orientation.
- Develop the actual decision: business should be learned from practitioners, through
  real work, near companies.
- End on the concrete campus and next-door teacher details.
- Do not append “Practitioners, real stakes, in the field,” “The classroom sits in the
  middle of the thing it teaches,” or an equivalent recap.

### Governance

- Board credentials must demonstrate why the governors are relevant to a practitioner-led
  institution, rather than serve as a list of biographies.
- For Manoj Kohli, customer growth already establishes scale in this time budget. Omit
  the Africa expansion unless a longer route gives it a distinct, developed purpose.
- Do not use “First, what this institution is” or similar fragments.
- The approved draft wording for this review is: “The Government of Haryana has passed
  the university-status bill for Masters' Union University.” This is a documented
  wording exception for clarity, not formal legal or PR approval.
- EFMD, AACSB, BGA, BSIS and NSDC are memberships or affiliations. Do not call them
  accreditations.

### Learn by doing and outcomes

- Keep a clear bridge between named successes, Shark Tank examples and the wider
  venture population before mentioning failures. Do not imply that PlaySuper or the
  televised startups failed.
- “Some of those ventures failed. The founders came out sharper, and most of them got
  hired.” refers to the broader population of ventures.
- Outcomes must be labelled as the postgraduate Cohort of 2025.
- Keep average and median together. Distinguish annual CTC from take-home pay.
- Treat 3.03x as a reported historical cohort average, not a listener promise.
- Preserve the whole-cohort split. Do not fold founders into placement figures.
- Named transitions are proof of changed functions; use people before aggregate numbers
  when the route provides both.

### Faculty

- The faculty composition is percentages, not headcounts: 40% practitioners, 30%
  full-time PhD faculty and 30% visiting international faculty.
- Each group needs a distinct role: why it works, how it is done elsewhere and how it
  is done in practice.
- International faculty teach full courses, not only guest lectures.

### Challenges, funds and labs

- The Reel Store story is about orders, returns, unsold stock and changing the sales
  channel to offline stalls and campus pop-ups. Do not call it a failure.
- Explain return on ad spend as a revenue-to-ad-spend measure. Do not imply profit.
- Keep the Masters' Union Investment Fund distinct from MU Ventures and venture grants.
- Food Lab is a lab in Section 4D. Never call it the Food Lab Challenge or place it in
  the challenges section.
- Food Lab's useful story begins with the obstacle: students with food ideas first had
  to solve for a kitchen, permits and delivery. The lab removes that obstacle so they
  can test brand, menu, sourcing, pricing, marketing, fulfilment and real revenue.
- Bloomberg Lab does not require a named student outcome. Its usefulness is established
  by a concrete supported sequence: a market headline and price move; investigation of
  news, fundamentals, flows and macro drivers; an investment thesis; assessment of the
  risk and what happens if the thesis is wrong. The student investment fund provides an
  earned callback because it runs on these terminals.
- Mention equipment only when its use advances the argument. Infrastructure lists are
  weak without the work students perform on them.

### Ventures and immersions

- Preserve the venture programme's staged sequence and distinguish grants, investor
  interest and funding.
- Capital interest is not completed investment.
- State the Founder's Fellowship terms exactly when selected; the placement return is
  a second opportunity to participate in the process, not a guaranteed job.
- Immersions need more than place names. Explain why different operating environments
  matter and retain the written brief-back to the host as the consequence of the visit.

### Close

- Explain the two TBM routes clearly.
- Use one admissions action and one Q&A invitation.
- Do not create an Admissions consultation, fit framework, deadline or link.

## Current approved story guidance

- **Lexi's:** it started with 150 sandwiches made in a hostel and brought to campus;
  Pratham was the first customer. Use Pratham because he participates in the story, not
  as a source attribution. Preserve the reported nature of the later sales figure.
- **Bloomberg Lab:** use the concrete market-investigation sequence above. A named
  student is optional enrichment, not a prerequisite.
- **Food Lab:** use the practical obstacle and operating sequence above. It is a lab,
  never a challenge.
- **The Reel Store:** preserve the change in sales channel caused by returned inventory.
  Its value lies in the decision under pressure.
- **EIGHT:** preserve the live prototype, launch within the batch, real usage and
  immediate feedback. Do not add invented adoption or funding outcomes.

## Rejected patterns for this route

- filler credentials whose significance cannot be developed;
- slogan-first opening;
- a corporate programme-fit frame;
- Hindi or Hinglish;
- repeated claims that students need not become founders;
- a maxim after every example;
- lists of facilities, faculty or companies without an argumentative role;
- stage directions mixed into spoken text;
- source citations spoken as part of the story;
- automatic claims that a model audit made the draft PR-approved.
